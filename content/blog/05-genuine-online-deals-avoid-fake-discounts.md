Big sale events promise huge discounts — "70% off!", "lowest price ever!" — but not every deal is what it seems. A discount is only meaningful if the **final price is genuinely lower for the product you actually want**. Here's how to tell.

## How "% off" is usually calculated

Most listings calculate the discount from the **MRP (maximum retail price)**:

> Discount % = (MRP − selling price) ÷ MRP × 100

The catch: many products routinely sell **below** their MRP. If a product normally sells at ₹1,800 with an MRP of ₹3,000, a "sale" price of ₹1,700 shows as "43% off" — but it's only ₹100 below the usual price.

**What to do:** compare the sale price with what the product **typically sells for**, not just the MRP.

## Check that it's the same product

Discounts sometimes apply to a slightly different item:

- an **older model** or previous generation,
- a **lower variant** (less storage, smaller size, fewer items in a pack),
- a **sale-specific version** with fewer features.

Match the **model number and variant** before celebrating the saving.

## Read the offer terms

Bank and payment offers can be valuable, but check:

| Term | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Eligible cards / payment methods | Do I have one? |
| Minimum order value | Does my order qualify? |
| Maximum discount cap | Is "10% off" capped at a small amount? |
| Instant vs later credit | Is the discount applied at checkout or credited later? |
| EMI conditions | Are there processing fees or interest? |

Only count discounts you **definitely qualify for** at checkout.

## Watch for pressure tactics

India's Central Consumer Protection Authority has issued guidelines against deceptive online design practices ("dark patterns"). Examples to watch for include:

- **False urgency** — countdowns or "only 1 left" messages designed to rush you.
- **Drip pricing** — extra charges revealed only at the final step.
- **Basket sneaking** — items or services added to your cart without your choice.
- **Bait and switch** — an advertised offer that isn't actually available when you try to buy.

If a deal only feels good because you're being rushed, step back and compare.

## Check the seller

During sales, many sellers list the same product. A slightly cheaper listing from an **unknown seller with poor ratings** may be riskier than a marginally higher price from the brand or an authorised seller — especially for electronics where warranty matters.

## Confirm the return and replacement policy

Some sale items are **non-returnable** or **replacement-only**. Make sure you're comfortable with the policy before you buy, particularly for clothing, footwear and gadgets.

## A simple way to judge a deal

1. **Decide what you need** before the sale starts.
2. **Note the usual price** of that exact model a week or two before.
3. **Compare the final checkout price** across stores on the day, including fees.
4. **Apply only guaranteed offers**.
5. **Check the seller, warranty and return policy**.
6. **Buy only if** the saving is real and the product meets your needs.

## Red flags

- A price **far below** every authorised seller.
- An **MRP that looks inflated** compared with similar products.
- Reviews that are **all extremely positive** and posted around the same time.
- Payment requested **outside the official app** or via a link sent on chat.
