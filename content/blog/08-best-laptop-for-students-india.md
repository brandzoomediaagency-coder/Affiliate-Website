The right laptop can carry you through an entire degree; the wrong one becomes frustratingly slow by the second year. The key is to **start from your course and daily routine**, not from the biggest numbers on a spec sheet.

## Step 1: Start with your course

| Course / use | What matters most | Sensible starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arts, commerce, humanities | Battery, keyboard, portability | Mid-range processor, 8GB RAM, 512GB SSD |
| Computer science / coding | RAM, processor, keyboard | 16GB RAM preferred, 512GB SSD |
| Engineering (CAD, simulation) | Processor, RAM, sometimes a GPU | Check software requirements; 16GB RAM |
| Design, video, architecture | Display accuracy, RAM, GPU | Colour-accurate screen, 16GB RAM |
| Online classes and notes | Webcam, mic, battery | Full HD display, good battery |

Check your **department's recommended software and system requirements** before buying.

## Processor

- Choose a **recent generation** — very old chips in new laptops age quickly.
- **Low-power (U/P-series or equivalent)** chips favour battery life and portability; **high-performance (H-series)** chips suit heavy software but use more power.
- Compare real-world performance in **independent reviews** rather than core counts alone.

## RAM (memory)

- **8GB** handles everyday study work.
- **16GB** gives breathing room for coding, design and many browser tabs.
- Check if RAM is **upgradeable** or **soldered**. If soldered, buy enough upfront.

## Storage (SSD)

- An **SSD is essential** for speed. Avoid hard-disk-only laptops.
- **512GB** is a comfortable minimum; 256GB fills up quickly with software and projects.
- Use cloud storage or an external drive for backups.

## Display

- **Full HD (1920×1080)** at minimum for sharp text.
- **IPS or OLED** panels offer better viewing angles than older TN panels.
- **Brightness** (often given in nits) matters in bright classrooms.
- Design students should look for good **colour-gamut coverage** in reviews.

## Battery life

Manufacturer figures are measured in ideal conditions. Look for **real-world tests** in reviews that reflect browsing and video calls. A laptop that lasts through a day of classes saves hunting for sockets.

## Portability and build

- Around **1.3–1.6 kg** is comfortable to carry daily.
- A **comfortable keyboard** and good trackpad matter for long assignments.
- Check **ports**: USB-C (ideally with charging), USB-A, HDMI and a headphone jack.

## Warranty and service

- Compare **carry-in vs on-site** warranty.
- Consider **accidental damage protection** if you commute a lot.
- Check the brand's **service centres** near your college or home.
- Keep the **GST invoice** safe.

## Making the budget work

- **Prioritise:** SSD and enough RAM matter more than a slightly faster processor.
- **Avoid paying for** a gaming GPU you don't need.
- **Check official student programmes** from brands and authorised retailers — terms change, so verify them on the brand's site.
- **Refurbished** laptops from reputable sellers with warranties can stretch a budget.
- Remember **software costs** (office suites, specialist tools) — many institutions provide licences.

## Student laptop checklist

- [ ] Course software requirements checked
- [ ] Recent processor, compared in reviews
- [ ] 8GB+ RAM (16GB for heavy work), upgradeability known
- [ ] 512GB SSD
- [ ] Full HD display with decent brightness
- [ ] Real-world battery life checked
- [ ] Weight and ports suit your routine
- [ ] Warranty and service network confirmed
