Most online shopping problems — the wrong variant, a fake product, a refused return — can be avoided with a few minutes of checking before you pay. Here are ten checks worth making every time.

## 1. Who is the seller?

On marketplaces, the platform and the seller are often different businesses. Look at:

- the **seller name** on the product page,
- **seller ratings and feedback**, and
- whether the seller is the **brand or an authorised seller** for expensive items.

Indian e-commerce rules require marketplaces to display seller details, so this information should be available on the listing.

## 2. Are the specifications exactly what you need?

Read beyond the title:

- **Model number and variant** (size, colour, capacity, RAM/storage).
- **Compatibility** — chargers, cases, spare parts and accessories must match your device.
- **What's in the box.**

If something is unclear, check the brand's official website.

## 3. What is the return policy?

Return terms vary by **category, seller and store**:

- Is it a refund, a replacement or an exchange?
- How many days do you have?
- Are there conditions (unopened, tags attached, original packaging)?

Some categories, such as innerwear or opened personal-care products, may be non-returnable.

## 4. What warranty do you get?

- **Brand warranty or seller warranty?** Brand warranties are usually easier to claim.
- **Duration and coverage** — some parts may be covered for longer than others.
- Make sure you'll get a **GST invoice**; it's typically needed for claims.

## 5. When will it arrive, and what will delivery cost?

Enter your pin code to see the **delivery estimate**. Check for **delivery fees** below a minimum order value and any cash-on-delivery charges.

## 6. Are you paying safely?

- Pay only inside the **official app or website** — never via links sent by strangers.
- **Never share OTPs, UPI PINs or card details** with anyone claiming to be support staff.
- A UPI **"collect request" takes money from you**; you never need to enter your PIN to *receive* money.

If you suspect fraud, call your bank and the **cybercrime helpline 1930**.

## 7. Does the product look authentic?

- Compare the listing photos and details with the **brand's official product page**.
- Be cautious of prices far below what authorised sellers charge.
- Many products sold in India must carry a **BIS certification mark** (such as the ISI mark or the Standard Mark with a registration number). You can verify certifications on the BIS website.
- Packaged products should show the **manufacturer or importer details, MRP and country of origin**.

## 8. Is the total price fair — and not above MRP?

Packaged products must not be sold above their **maximum retail price (MRP)**. Compare the final checkout price across stores, including fees, rather than relying on the "% off" badge. Our guide to [spotting genuine deals](/blog/genuine-online-deals-avoid-fake-discounts) explains how.

## 9. How will you get help if something goes wrong?

Check the store's **customer support options** and note the **grievance officer** details (marketplaces are required to publish them). If an issue isn't resolved:

1. Raise it through your order page and keep screenshots.
2. Escalate to the store's grievance officer.
3. Contact the **National Consumer Helpline (1915)** or file a complaint on **e-Daakhil**.

## 10. What do recent reviews actually say?

- Sort by **most recent** — products change over time.
- Prefer reviews with **photos, details and verified purchase** labels.
- Look for **patterns** (for example repeated complaints about battery life), not single angry or glowing reviews.
