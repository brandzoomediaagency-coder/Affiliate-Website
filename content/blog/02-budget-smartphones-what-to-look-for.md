A budget smartphone is a balancing act. Brands advertise big numbers — megapixels, "virtual RAM", charging watts — but the phone that serves you well for two or three years is the one that gets the **fundamentals** right.

This guide explains what each specification means in practice, so you can compare phones in your budget with confidence.

## Start with how you'll use the phone

Before comparing specs, decide what matters most to you:

| If you mostly… | Prioritise |
| --- | --- |
| Use social media, calls and video | Display quality, battery life, reliable software |
| Take lots of photos | Main camera quality, stabilisation, sample photos in low light |
| Play games | Processor, cooling, display refresh rate |
| Keep phones for 3+ years | Software update policy, RAM, storage, service network |

## Display

You look at the display all day, so it's worth getting right:

- **Size and resolution.** Full HD+ (1080p) looks noticeably sharper than HD+ (720p) on larger screens.
- **Panel type.** AMOLED panels offer deeper blacks and are common at higher budget levels; good LCDs can also be pleasant.
- **Refresh rate.** 90Hz or 120Hz makes scrolling feel smoother, but only matters if the processor can keep up.
- **Brightness.** Higher peak brightness helps outdoors in Indian sunlight. Check reviews, as brand figures are often peak values.

## Processor (chipset)

The processor determines how smooth the phone feels and how well it ages.

- Compare **chipset names**, not just "octa-core" — most phones are octa-core.
- Look at **independent reviews** for real-world performance and heat.
- Very old chipsets in new phones may feel slow sooner and receive fewer updates.

## RAM and storage

- **RAM:** 6GB is workable for everyday use; 8GB offers more headroom. "Virtual" or "extended" RAM borrows storage and isn't equivalent to physical RAM.
- **Storage:** 128GB is a sensible minimum for most people today. Check whether there's a **microSD slot** if you need more.
- **Variants:** the same phone is often sold in several RAM/storage combinations at different prices — always compare the same variant.

## Camera

More megapixels don't automatically mean better photos. What matters:

- **Main camera quality** — look at real sample photos in daylight, indoors and at night.
- **Stabilisation** (OIS or EIS) for videos and low light.
- **Secondary lenses.** A good ultra-wide is useful; low-resolution macro or depth sensors usually add little.

## Battery and charging

- **Capacity (mAh)** is only part of the story; processor efficiency and software matter too. Look for real-world battery tests.
- **Charging speed** and whether a **charger is included in the box**.
- A **USB-C** port is standard on modern phones.

## Software updates

This is often overlooked but matters if you keep phones for years:

- Check the brand's **official commitment** to Android version upgrades and security patches for that specific model.
- Fewer pre-installed apps and ads make day-to-day use cleaner.

## 5G support

Most new phones support 5G, but **band support varies**. Indian operators use specific 5G bands, so compare the phone's supported bands (listed on the brand's official spec page) with those your operator supports.

## Build, durability and warranty

- An **IP rating** (for example IP54 or IP64) indicates splash or dust resistance — it's not a guarantee against water damage.
- Check the **warranty period**, what it covers, and whether the brand has **service centres** near you.
- Buy from authorised sellers where possible, and keep the **GST invoice**.

## Buyer checklist

- [ ] Same model and exact RAM/storage variant compared
- [ ] Chipset checked in independent reviews
- [ ] Display resolution and brightness suit your use
- [ ] Battery life tested in reviews, charger situation clear
- [ ] Brand's update policy confirmed for this model
- [ ] 5G bands match your operator
- [ ] Warranty, service network and seller checked
- [ ] IMEI verified after purchase via Sanchar Saathi
