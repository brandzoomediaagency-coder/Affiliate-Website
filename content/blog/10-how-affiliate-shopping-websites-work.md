If you've ever clicked a "Buy Now" button on a review or comparison website and landed on Amazon or Flipkart, you've used an **affiliate link**. This guide explains, transparently, how that works — including how websites like ours are funded.

## What is affiliate marketing?

Affiliate marketing is a partnership in which a **merchant pays a website (the affiliate) a commission** when a visitor the website referred makes a qualifying purchase.

- The **merchant** gets customers it might not otherwise reach.
- The **affiliate** earns income to fund its content.
- The **shopper** gets help discovering and comparing products — at no extra cost.

## What is an affiliate link?

An affiliate link is a normal link to the merchant's website that includes a **tracking identifier** unique to the affiliate. For example, a programme may add a tag or ID to the URL, or issue a short link that redirects to the merchant with that identifier attached.

When you click the link, the merchant can tell which affiliate referred you.

## How tracking works

1. You click an affiliate link on a website.
2. The merchant's site records the referral — typically using the tracking ID in the link and a cookie in your browser.
3. If you buy a **qualifying product** within the programme's attribution window, the merchant credits the affiliate.
4. The merchant reports the sale and the commission to the affiliate.

Each programme sets its **own rules** — which products qualify, how long the attribution window lasts, and what counts as a valid referral.

## Who do you buy from?

Always the **merchant**. When you click through:

- you see the **merchant's price and stock**,
- you **check out on the merchant's website**,
- the merchant handles **payment, delivery, returns, refunds and warranty**.

The affiliate website doesn't take payment or process orders.

## How commissions work

- Commissions are usually a **percentage of the sale value** and vary by programme and product category.
- They're paid by the **merchant from its margin**, so **your price doesn't change**.
- Commissions are paid only for **qualifying purchases** — cancelled or returned orders typically don't count.
- A **click is not a sale**. Most clicks don't lead to a purchase.

## Transparency and disclosures

Trustworthy affiliate websites are open about how they earn money:

- **Clear disclosures** near affiliate links and on a dedicated page.
- **Programme-required statements** — for example, Amazon Associates requires the statement *"As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."*
- In India, advertising guidelines from the Advertising Standards Council of India emphasise disclosing material connections.
- **Editorial independence** — recommendations shouldn't be decided by commission rates.

## Affiliate websites vs cashback websites

| | Affiliate discovery website | Cashback website |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earns commission from merchants | Yes | Yes |
| Shares commission with shoppers | No | Yes, as cashback or points |
| Wallet or withdrawal system | No | Usually |
| Main value | Discovery, comparison, guides | Rewards on purchases |

We're a **discovery website**: we don't offer cashback, reward points, coins or wallet balances.

## How visitors benefit

- **Discovery** — curated categories and products instead of endless scrolling.
- **Comparison** — see which stores list a product, with prices when verified.
- **Guidance** — buying guides that explain what to check before you spend.
- **Choice** — you decide where to buy, and you always buy directly from the store.

## The bottom line

Affiliate links let product discovery websites stay free to use. You pay the merchant's normal price, you buy from the merchant, and the website may earn a small commission if you make a qualifying purchase. Learn more on [how our website works](/how-it-works).
